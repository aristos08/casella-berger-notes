Statistical Inference — Course Notes Casella & Berger as the mathematical spine, taught computationally

A measure-theoretic, proof-driven walk through Casella & Berger (2nd ed.), with every major result tied to a seeded simulation and real-world intuition. Written for a reader comfortable through real analysis and measure-theoretic probability.

How these notes work

  • Rigor. Full proofs at the level of someone who has done measure theory; the measure-theoretic scaffolding (product measures, pushforwards, sub-σ-algebras) is made explicit rather than hidden.
  • Computation. Each major claim ships with a Simulation Check box reporting real output from a seeded, pure-Python standard-library simulation — empirical quantities next to their theoretical targets.
  • Intuition. Why each idea matters, where it breaks, and how it shows up in quantitative / ML / experimentation work.

Chapters

We begin at Chapter 5 (Chapters 1–4 are assumed). Chapters fill in one session at a time; each lives in its own folder with an HTML page and a LaTeX twin.

5. Properties of a Random Sample done

Random samples, sums & MGFs, normal sampling theory, order statistics, convergence concepts, delta method, random generation.

6. Principles of Data Reduction todo

Sufficiency, minimal sufficiency, ancillarity, completeness, likelihood & equivariance principles.

7. Point Estimation todo

Method of moments, MLE, Bayes, EM; MSE, UMVUE, Cramér–Rao, Rao–Blackwell, loss optimality.

8. Hypothesis Testing todo

LRTs, Bayesian & UI/IU tests; power, Neyman–Pearson, p-values, loss optimality.

9. Interval Estimation todo

Inverting tests, pivots, pivoting the CDF, Bayesian intervals; coverage & optimality.

10. Asymptotic Evaluations todo

Consistency, efficiency, bootstrap SEs, robustness, M-estimators, large-sample tests & intervals.

11. ANOVA & Regression todo

Oneway ANOVA, contrasts, the F test, simple linear regression, BLUE, prediction.

12. Regression Models todo

Errors-in-variables, logistic regression, robust regression.
