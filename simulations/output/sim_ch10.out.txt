10.2.1 ROBUSTNESS: 10% contamination from N(0,100) destroys the mean
========================================================================
  n=50, mixture 0.9*N(0,1) + 0.1*N(0,100); true centre 0.  MSE over 60000 reps:
     mean        : MSE=0.21873  sd=0.46769
     median      : MSE=0.03745  sd=0.19352
     20% trimmed : MSE=0.02939  sd=0.17144
  (for comparison, with NO contamination the mean's MSE would be 1/n = 0.0200)
  -> the mean's variance is inflated by the 10% of draws with sd 10; the median and
     trimmed mean barely notice. Breakdown point: mean 0, median 0.5, 20% trimmed 0.2.

10.3.2 WALD vs SCORE vs LRT: three asymptotically equivalent tests, three finite-n sizes
========================================================================
  Bernoulli, H0: p=p0 vs two-sided, nominal 5%.  EXACT sizes by summation over x.
  Note: at x=0 or x=n the Wald statistic is 0/0. Treating it as a rejection (statistic

    0.50  |    50 |  0.0649  |  0.0649  |  0.0649  |  0.0649  |   0.0000

    0.10  |    20 |  0.1240  |  0.0024  |  0.0432  |  0.1328  |   0.1216
    0.10  |    50 |  0.1211  |  0.1159  |  0.0297  |  0.0583  |   0.0052
    0.10  |   200 |  0.0729  |  0.0729  |  0.0439  |  0.0591  |   0.0000
    0.03  |    20 |  0.5441  |  0.0003  |  0.0210  |  0.0210  |   0.5438

========================================================================
10.1.4 ... AND FAILS for the maximum: the bootstrap cannot resample an extreme
========================================================================
  n=50: P(a bootstrap resample contains the sample max) = 0.6347
        exact 1-(1-1/n)^n = 0.6358   limit 1-1/e = 0.6321
  So the bootstrap distribution of the max has an atom of mass about 0.632 at the
  observed max. For Uniform(0,theta) the true limit law of n(theta - X_(n))/theta is
  Exponential(1), which is continuous -- so the bootstrap limit is simply the wrong law.
  true pivot n(theta-X_(n)): P(=0)=0.0000, median=0.6909 (Exp(1) median 0.6931)
  bootstrap pivot n(X_(n)-X*_(n)): P(=0)=0.6328  <- a point mass that should not be there

========================================================================
10.4 LARGE-SAMPLE INTERVALS: Wilson IS the inverted score test (and that is why it wins)
========================================================================
  max |Wilson endpoint - inverted-score endpoint| over x=0..20: 4.441e-16

  n=20, nominal 95%, EXACT coverage over p in (0.001,0.999):
     method            |  min cov  |  mean cov  |  mean length
     Wald              |  0.0198   |   0.8467   |    0.3243
     Score (Wilson)    |  0.8516   |   0.9532   |    0.3255
     LRT-inverted      |  0.8233   |   0.9465   |    0.3220
     Clopper-Pearson   |  0.9581   |   0.9770   |    0.3663
  -> the Wald interval uses the variance estimated AT the estimate; the score interval
     uses the variance under each candidate p0, which is correct under that hypothesis.
     That single difference explains Chapter 9's finding.
